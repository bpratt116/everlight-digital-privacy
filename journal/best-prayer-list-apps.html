The best prayer list apps in 2026

November 2026

A prayer list is a simple idea: write down who and what you promised to pray for, then actually pray, and check back. The apps that do this well are small and focused. The apps that get the most downloads are often something else, like guided audio, Bible reading, or meditation. Both kinds can be good. They just solve different problems.

We looked at nine iPhone apps that people reach for when they want to pray more intentionally. Here is what each one does well, what it costs, and who should look elsewhere.

Full disclosure: we make Pilgrim: Prayer List. We've tried to be fair — every app here is good at something.

How we picked

  • It helps you pray, not just store notes. We looked for a way to work through requests, not only record them.
  • It is still maintained and on the App Store today. We checked each listing in November 2026.
  • The free version is usable. You should be able to find out if it fits before paying anything.
  • It respects different traditions. Some apps here are Catholic, some are evangelical, some are liturgical. We note the leaning and leave the judging to you.

Quick picks

  • Best for a private, no-account prayer list: Pilgrim: Prayer List
  • Best for scheduling and variety: PrayerMate
  • Best for praying alongside others: Echo Prayer
  • Best for a simple free list: Prayer Notebook
  • Best for reminders through the day: Prayminder
  • Best for guided Catholic prayer: Hallow
  • Best for a daily liturgy-style rhythm: Lectio 365
  • Best for meditation and Bible audio: Abide
  • Best if you already read in YouVersion: YouVersion Bible App

1. Pilgrim: Prayer List

Best for: keeping a private list of people and needs, praying through it, and remembering what God answered.
Price: free for your first 10 active prayers. Plus is optional and is offered monthly, yearly, or as a one-time lifetime purchase.

You add a request in a few seconds, with who it is for, what they asked, and when, and you can pick a name straight from Contacts. A prayer session walks you through your list one request at a time. When something is answered, you mark it and add a note of thanks. Answered prayers don't count against the free limit of 10, and they stay in their own tab. There is no account and no ads, and prayers stay on your device, or in your own private iCloud storage if you turn sync on. Plus adds named lists, a People view, a Home Screen widget, and a year in review. Our app page has the details.

Skip it if: you want guided audio prayers or a library of devotional content. It is a list and a prayer session, not a devotional.

2. PrayerMate

Best for: people who want the app to decide what to pray for each day.
Price: free. Optional Patron subscription from about $0.99 a month, which unlocks extra color schemes, plus optional paid prayer packs.

PrayerMate shows each day's prayer topics as swipeable cards, drawn from the categories you set up. You can schedule by day, by date, or by time since you last prayed for something. It can also subscribe you to prayer updates from charities and churches, and includes a daily Operation World country feed. It runs on iPhone and iPad. See it on the App Store.

Skip it if: you want a clean, minimal screen. It has a lot of options, and that suits some people better than others.

3. Echo Prayer

Best for: praying with a small group or following ministry requests.
Price: free to start. Echo Plus+ is a monthly or yearly subscription with a 14-day free trial; check the app for current prices.

Echo has a prayer list with categories and answered prayers, reminders by push notification or email, and a "Pray Now" mode with an optional timer. Its standout is sharing: you can share requests privately with individuals or groups, and let someone know you prayed for them. Churches and ministries can also post updates through Echo Feeds. Visit echoprayer.com.

Skip it if: you want a purely solo list with no sharing features in the way.

4. Prayer Notebook

Best for: a simple, free prayer list with a full-screen prayer mode.
Price: free.

Prayer Notebook lets you organize prayers by category, attach a contact or a photo, and schedule them daily, weekly, monthly, or on a set date. Prayer Mode shows your list full-screen for focused time. You can mark requests answered and message a contact after you pray for them. It runs on iPhone and iPad. See the App Store listing.

Skip it if: you use more than one device. The listing says syncing between devices is not available in the current version.

5. Prayminder

Best for: getting reminders spread through the day.
Price: free.

Prayminder sends reminders at times you choose for the prayers, verses, or words you add. You can group them (family, church, work), attach Bible verses, read the Bible in the app with translations from YouVersion, and view stats on your history. It works without an account and stores data on your device. Learn more at prayminder.com.

Skip it if: you want guided prayers or a library of devotional content. It is built around reminders for what you add.

6. Hallow

Best for: guided, audio-led prayer in the Catholic tradition, open to anyone.
Price: some prayer content is free. The full app lists at $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year in the US.

Hallow offers the Rosary, Lectio Divina on the daily readings, the Divine Mercy Chaplet, the Ignatian Examen, novenas, and seasonal programs for Lent and Advent. Sessions come in 5, 10, or 15 minutes, with journaling afterward. It also has Hallow Family groups for sharing intentions. See hallow.com.

Skip it if: you mainly want a list of people to pray for. Hallow is a content library first.

7. Lectio 365

Best for: a daily rhythm of morning, midday, and night prayer.
Price: free.

From the 24-7 Prayer movement, Lectio 365 gives you a short guided prayer three times a day. Morning follows a Pause, Rejoice, Ask, Yield pattern inspired by Lectio Divina, and night prayer draws on the Ignatian Examen. You can listen or read, download a week ahead, and use it in English, Portuguese, Simplified Chinese, or Spanish. See lectio365.com.

Skip it if: you want to keep your own list of requests. The content is the same for every user.

8. Abide

Best for: Christian meditation, Bible audio, and bedtime content.
Price: free to download. Abide Premium is a paid subscription with a free trial; prices vary, so check the app.

Abide offers over 2,000 meditations, NIV Bible audio, bedtime stories, favorites, and a journal, with sessions of two to fifteen minutes. It can log mindful minutes to Apple Health. Visit abide.com.

Skip it if: you need a prayer list. Abide is built for listening, not for tracking requests.

9. YouVersion Bible App

Best for: people who already read the Bible in YouVersion and want prayer in the same place.
Price: free.

YouVersion is a full Bible app with thousands of versions, audio, and reading plans. Its prayer features include prayer cards and lists, which you can keep private or share with friends, plus a way to celebrate answered prayers. Details at bible.com.

Skip it if: you want a dedicated prayer tool. Prayer is one part of a much larger app.

How to choose

Start with what you want to do. If it is to remember people, pick a list app: Pilgrim: Prayer List, PrayerMate, Echo Prayer, Prayer Notebook, or Prayminder. If it is to be led in prayer, look at Hallow, Lectio 365, or Abide. If you want everything under one roof, YouVersion is hard to beat for the price.

Then check three things: whether your data syncs across devices, whether it needs an account, and what the paid tier adds. Try two apps for a week each. A good prayer app is one you still open in month three. For ideas on what to put in your list, see building a prayer list.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free prayer app?

For a plain, free prayer list, Prayer Notebook, Prayminder, and Echo Prayer are all free to download and cover the basics. Pilgrim: Prayer List is also free, with up to 10 active prayers and a paid Plus option if you need more. The best one is the one that matches how you actually pray.

What is the best app for keeping a prayer list?

Look for an app built around a list of people and requests rather than daily devotional content. PrayerMate, Echo Prayer, Prayer Notebook, Prayminder, and Pilgrim: Prayer List are all designed that way.

Is there a prayer app that doesn't need an account?

Yes. Pilgrim: Prayer List and Prayminder both say they work without an account and keep your data on your device. Check each app's privacy details before you start.

Can I track answered prayers in an app?

Yes. Pilgrim: Prayer List, Echo Prayer, Prayer Notebook, and YouVersion all let you mark prayers as answered.
