The 10 best Bible apps in 2026

November 2026

There are hundreds of Bible apps, and most people only need one or two. The right one depends on how you read: a chapter before bed, a deep dive for Sunday's passage, or something you listen to on the drive. This list covers ten apps that each do one thing well, with current prices and an honest note on who each is not for.

Full disclosure: we make Pilgrim. We've tried to be fair — every app here is good at something.

How we picked

  • It exists and is maintained. Every app here is on the iOS App Store today.
  • It does one thing clearly. Reading, study, audio, or prayer. Not everything at once.
  • Pricing is plain. You can tell what is free and what costs money.
  • Range. We included Protestant, Catholic and study-focused options so most readers find a fit.

Quick picks

  • Best for quiet daily reading: Pilgrim
  • Best for community and reach: YouVersion
  • Best for serious study: Logos
  • Best modular study Bible: Olive Tree
  • Best for listening: Dwell
  • Best free word study: Blue Letter Bible
  • Best for ESV readers: ESV Bible
  • Best for translation variety: Bible Gateway
  • Best Catholic prayer app: Hallow
  • Best devotional habit: Glorify

1. Pilgrim

Best for: quiet, free daily reading with a friend
Price: Free, no ads, no in-app purchases (an account is optional).

Pilgrim is ours, so weigh our opinion accordingly. It gives you 15 translations, including ESV, CSB, NIV, NLT, NASB, NKJV, NET, LEB and KJV, with side-by-side comparison. Matthew Henry and John Gill sit beside the verse as commentary, and you can read a plan with a spouse or friend and share notes on each day. It also includes the Heidelberg and Westminster catechisms, Bible memory cards, and a 75 Day Challenge. Missed days stay open instead of resetting.

Skip it if: you want an audio Bible, a kids mode, or a full original-language study library. Pilgrim has none of those. Prayer lists now live in a separate app, Pilgrim: Prayer List.

2. YouVersion Bible App

Best for: reach and community
Price: Free.

YouVersion is the most-installed Bible app in the world, with over 700 million installs, 3,500+ Bible versions and about 2,300 languages. It covers reading, audio, plans, prayer and sharing with friends, and offline access works well. If your church or small group is already on it, that alone is a strong reason to stay.

Skip it if: you want a quieter screen. It does a lot, and some people find that busy.

3. Logos

Best for: serious study and sermon prep
Price: Free app; Logos Plus about $4.99/month or $49.99/year, Premium about $9.99/month or $99.99/year (App Store).

Logos is built around a large library of commentaries, Factbook background on people and places, and AI-assisted tools like Study Assistant and Smart Search that answer from your own books. The free version includes over 40 Bibles and books. Subscriptions add more AI use, more books and discounts.

Skip it if: you just want to read a chapter and close the app. The depth is the point, and it can feel like a lot.

4. Olive Tree Bible App

Best for: a modular study Bible on iPhone and iPad
Price: Free app with five translations (ESV, NIV, KJV, NKJV, CSB); a Starter Pack from about $2.99/month, and Study Packs for ESV, NIV or NKJV about $7.99/month (App Store).

Olive Tree lets you add the resources you want: a Resource Guide that links commentaries and notes to your text, parallel views, Strong's popups, and Greek and Hebrew tools. Smart Search finds passages from plain questions, and notes and highlights sync across devices. It works offline and supports iPhone, iPad and Apple Vision.

Skip it if: you would rather not shop for add-ons. Premium translations and study notes are bought separately.

5. Dwell

Best for: listening to Scripture
Price: Free to start; $59.99/year or $9.99/month after a 7-day trial.

Dwell is an audio Bible with over 20 voice recordings and 14 translations, made for commutes, chores and bedtime. The recordings are the main attraction, and the app is built around them.

Skip it if: you mostly read. The subscription is for listening, and a reader will not get its full value.

6. Blue Letter Bible

Best for: free word study
Price: Free.

Blue Letter Bible ties original-language tools to every verse: a Hebrew and Greek lexicon, concordances, dictionaries, the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge, and advanced word searches. It carries over 30 translations, with CSB installed and ESV, LSB and NASB free to download, plus audio Bibles and audio commentaries.

Skip it if: you want a polished devotional feel. It is a study tool first.

7. ESV Bible

Best for: ESV readers who like to listen
Price: Free; an optional subscription unlocks more study resources.

Crossway's own app is a clean reader with cross-references, over 50 reading plans, and audio read by voices like Kristyn Getty and Ray Ortlund, with optional instrumental background music. The ESV Global Study Bible is free. A subscription adds the ESV Study Bible and commentary series.

Skip it if: you want to switch between many translations. It is built around the ESV.

8. Bible Gateway

Best for: translation variety
Price: Free; Bible Gateway Plus $6.99/month or $69.99/year, with a 7-day trial.

Bible Gateway was rebuilt in spring 2026. It offers 200+ translations, 30+ audio Bibles, plans, notes and highlights that sync with a free account. Plus removes ads and adds 60+ study resources, including study Bibles and commentaries.

Skip it if: you want ad-free reading without paying. Ad-free is a Plus feature.

9. Hallow

Best for: Catholic prayer and meditation
Price: Free version; $69.99/year or $9.99/month, plus a Friends and Family plan at $119.99/year.

Hallow is a Catholic prayer app with over 10,000 audio-guided sessions, including meditation and Catholic Bible readings. It is a prayer app more than a Bible reader, and it does that job very well for Catholic users.

Skip it if: you want a Bible reader with many translations, or you are not looking for Catholic content.

10. Glorify

Best for: a daily devotional habit
Price: Free to download; Glorify Plus from about $7/month (App Store listing).

Glorify starts your day with a quote, a Bible passage and a short devotional. It adds prayer tools, worship music, courses and community features for sharing prayer requests with friends or a church.

Skip it if: you want a full Bible reader first. The devotional is the center of the app, not the text.

A word on prices and traditions

Subscription prices move, and the App Store listing is the final word on what you will pay. Translations also differ by tradition. Catholic readers will want a Bible that includes the deuterocanonical books, and Protestant readers often care most about their church's preferred translation. Check that your translation is available before you commit time to any app.

How to choose

Start with how you actually read. If you read a little most days, pick the simplest app that has your translation. If you study for teaching, pick a study platform and accept the learning curve. If you listen more than you read, start with an audio app. Prices above came from each app's own site or App Store listing in November 2026 and can change, so check before you subscribe. Two apps can coexist fine: many people keep one reader and one study tool. A free trial is the cheapest way to test whether an app fits your routine. Give it a week of your real mornings, not a single evening of tapping around.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free Bible app?

YouVersion, Bible Gateway, Blue Letter Bible, the ESV Bible app and Pilgrim are all free to use for reading. YouVersion has the widest reach, Blue Letter Bible the most free study tools, and Pilgrim has no ads and no in-app purchases.

What is the best Bible app for study?

Logos and Olive Tree are the deepest options, with commentaries, original-language tools and search. Blue Letter Bible offers free Hebrew and Greek tools.

Which Bible app has an audio Bible?

Dwell is built around audio. YouVersion, Blue Letter Bible, the ESV Bible app and Bible Gateway also include audio Bibles. Pilgrim does not have an audio Bible.

Is there a good Catholic Bible app?

Hallow is a Catholic prayer app with Catholic Bible readings and guided sessions. It is primarily a prayer app, so some Catholic readers pair it with a full Bible reader.
