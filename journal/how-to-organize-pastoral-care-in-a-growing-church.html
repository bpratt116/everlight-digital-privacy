How to Organize Pastoral Care in a Growing Church

July 2026

As a church grows, the pastoral team often hits a wall. What used to be a manageable task of keeping up with everyone becomes overwhelming. The personal touch that defined the early days starts to slip, and pastors worry that people are falling through the cracks. Organizing pastoral care in a growing church is one of the most critical challenges a leadership team will face.

The Breaking Point of Memory and Spreadsheets

In smaller congregations, pastoral care relies heavily on the pastor's memory. A quick conversation in the lobby about an upcoming surgery is easily remembered and followed up on. But as the congregation scales, human memory reaches its limits. Pastors turn to spreadsheets, sticky notes, or generic church management software (ChMS) to keep track of their flock.

The problem is that spreadsheets aren't built for care. They are built for data. When a pastor needs to know who is hurting, who needs a phone call, or who hasn't been engaged in months, a spreadsheet feels cold and cumbersome. Church member tracking tools need to be designed specifically for the nuanced, sensitive nature of pastoral care management.

Building a Care Strategy That Scales

To successfully organize pastoral care in a growing church, you need a strategy that distributes the load and provides the right tools for the job. Here are three steps to get started:

1. Move from a Solo Effort to a Team Approach

Pastoral care cannot rest on the shoulders of one person. Growing churches must develop a care team—elders, deacons, or trained volunteers—who share the responsibility. This means assigning "flocks" to specific leaders so that every member has a designated point of contact.

2. Standardize How You Capture Care Needs

Whether it's a prayer request submitted online, a hospital visit, or a casual conversation, there must be a central place to record it. The goal is to make capturing a note as frictionless as possible. If a pastor has to log into a clunky ChMS and navigate five menus just to record a visit, it won't happen. The system needs to be mobile-friendly and instantaneous.

3. Use Pastoral Care Software Built for the Job

Generic CRMs and administrative ChMS platforms fail because they prioritize records over relationships. A dedicated pastoral care software solution, like Shepherd, is built entirely around the pastoral workflow. It allows you to quickly log notes, assign follow-ups, manage prayer requests, and securely share sensitive information with the care team.

Keeping the Personal Touch

Scaling pastoral care doesn't mean becoming impersonal. It means building the infrastructure necessary so that pastors can focus on what they do best: caring for people. When you organize pastoral care effectively, no one is forgotten, and every member feels seen and valued.
