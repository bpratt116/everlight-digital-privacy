Why we type our schedules instead of tapping them

October 2026

Try setting up a meeting on your phone right now. You tap the plus icon in the corner. You type the title. Then you tap the start date, scroll through a three-column wheel of month, day, and year, and tap done. Then you tap the start time, scroll through hours, scroll through minutes, check whether it defaulted to AM or PM. Then you do the same thing for the end time. Then you open the repeat menu, scroll down to weekly, and choose an alert from a dropdown. By the time you hit “Save,” you’ve made twelve distinct taps, navigated three submenus, and completely forgotten what you were thinking about before you opened the app.

The time-wheel picker is a masterpiece of friction. It treats entering an event like programming a VCR in 1994. Yet when you talk to someone in real life, you never speak in dropdown menus. You say: “Let’s grab coffee next Thursday at 2:00 at the diner.” You gave the who, the what, the day, the time, and the place in nine words. Why can’t your calendar understand that?

Parsing as you type

When I started building Chrona, the goal wasn’t to reinvent the calendar grid — month views and day timelines have worked well for centuries. The goal was to eliminate the administrative toll of getting an event onto the grid in the first place.

Natural language parsing turns a sentence into structured calendar data in real time. You don’t wait for a cloud server or an AI bot to think about it; the parser runs directly on your device with every keystroke. Type “Staff meeting every Tuesday 9am to 10:30am alert 15m before” and you can see the event construct itself live beneath your fingers. The date locks in. The recurrence rule sets itself. The alert is attached. You hit Return, and you’re done. Two seconds instead of thirty.

Fusing tasks with the calendar

There’s a second, subtler friction that traditional calendar apps create: the wall between events and tasks. Most people keep their schedule in a calendar app and their to-do list in a completely separate task manager. The problem is that your day only has one timeline.

When your task list lives in isolation, you can stare at twenty items and convince yourself you’ll get them all done before dinner. Then you look at your calendar and realize you have five hours of back-to-back meetings. Your to-do list is writing checks your schedule can’t cash. Chrona puts tasks and events on the same screen. You can drag a task directly onto your afternoon timeline, give it twenty minutes of real estate, and see what your day actually looks like — honest, unhurried, and grounded in real time.

Your data stays on your device

A calendar holds some of the most private details of your life: doctor appointments, family dinners, confidential one-on-ones, travel plans. The last thing you need is another startup asking you to create an account, import your Google or iCloud calendars to their cloud database, and trust their security practices.

Chrona doesn’t have user accounts. It reads and writes the calendars already configured on your iPhone, iPad, or Mac via Apple’s native EventKit framework. If you use iCloud, Google Calendar, or Fastmail, your events stay right where they are. Your tasks sync across your devices via your own private iCloud account. Nothing touches an Everlight server, because Everlight doesn’t have a server for your schedule.

Software shouldn’t demand patience for routine tasks. It should do the obvious thing as quickly and quietly as possible, so you can stop looking at your calendar and start living the day you planned.
