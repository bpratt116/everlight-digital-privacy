The 10 best calendar apps for iPhone in 2026

November 2026

The calendar on your iPhone is probably the app you open most. The right one can save you real time, and the wrong one turns every new event into a dozen taps. We looked at ten calendar apps for iPhone that are worth your attention this year, from the one already on your phone to planners built for time-blocking.

Full disclosure: we make Chrona. We've tried to be fair — every app here is good at something.

How we picked

  • Everyday speed. How quickly can you get an event or a task into the app?
  • Works with what you have. Does it sync with iCloud, Google or Exchange calendars you already use?
  • Honest pricing. What is free, and what do you pay for?
  • Still available on iOS. We confirmed each app is listed on the App Store.

Quick picks

  • Best for typing events in plain English: Chrona
  • Best for a polished paid calendar: Fantastical
  • Best for zero setup: Apple Calendar
  • Best for Google users: Google Calendar
  • Best for work email: Microsoft Outlook
  • Best for an all-in-one planner: Calendars by Readdle
  • Best for daily timeline planning: Structured
  • Best for Notion users: Notion Calendar
  • Best for families and groups: TimeTree
  • Best for time-blocking tasks: Akiflow

1. Chrona

Best for: Typing events in plain English, with tasks and habits in the same app
Price: Free to use. Chrona Pro is $1.99 a month, $19.99 a year (7-day free trial).

Chrona is ours, so weigh this entry accordingly. You type a sentence like “Lunch with Sam Friday 1pm” and it becomes an event before you finish typing. It reads and writes the Google, iCloud and subscribed calendars already on your phone, adds a task system (projects, tags, priorities, an inbox), habit tracking, and weather in your reminders, and it runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac. There is no Chrona account; your data syncs through your own iCloud.

Skip it if: you want a team scheduling tool or a calendar that lives on Windows or Android. The full task system, habits and time-blocking are part of Pro. More: Chrona on the App Store.

2. Fantastical

Best for: People who want a polished, feature-rich calendar and are happy to pay for it
Price: Free tier; Premium is a paid subscription (check Flexibits for the current price).

Fantastical is the app that made natural-language event entry popular, and it is still a standout. It works with the calendars you already have, includes tasks, and runs on Mac, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Apple Vision Pro and Windows. The free plan covers the basics; Premium adds things like push sync and calendar mirroring.

Skip it if: you want everything in one price with no tiers to compare, or you only need a simple calendar view. More: Fantastical.

3. Apple Calendar

Best for: A no-install, no-fuss calendar that works with iCloud
Price: Free; built into iPhone.

It is already on your phone, it syncs through iCloud, and it connects to Google, Exchange and other accounts you add. Siri, widgets and Apple Watch support are built in. For many people it is all they need, and nothing else in this list is as tightly woven into iOS.

Skip it if: you want natural-language entry, a built-in task and habit system, or advanced planning views. More: Apple Calendar and iCloud.

4. Google Calendar

Best for: Best if your life already runs on Google
Price: Free (the App Store listing shows the app as free).

Google Calendar is the default for anyone using Gmail and Google Workspace. It can add events from Gmail such as flights and reservations, supports multiple calendars, widgets and tasks, and works across phone, web and Android. Sharing with other Google users is straightforward.

Skip it if: you are not in the Google ecosystem, or you would rather keep your schedule out of a Google account. More: Google Calendar.

5. Microsoft Outlook

Best for: Work email and calendar in one app
Price: Free app; Microsoft 365 is an optional paid upgrade.

Outlook puts your inboxes and calendars together, which suits anyone whose schedule is driven by work email. It makes it easy to share your availability and find meeting times, and it handles Exchange and Microsoft 365 accounts well. The app is free, with premium features tied to a Microsoft 365 subscription.

Skip it if: you only want a calendar. Bundling email in makes the app heavier than a calendar-only tool. More: Outlook.

6. Calendars by Readdle

Best for: An all-in-one planner with tasks and habit tracking
Price: Free to download; optional subscription for premium features.

Readdle describes Calendars as a calendar, task manager, daily planner and habit tracker in one. It syncs with Google, Outlook/Exchange and iCloud, accepts natural-language input, and runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. Premium covers AI assistance, unlimited habit tracking and extra customization.

Skip it if: you want everything unlocked without a subscription, or you only need a bare-bones calendar. More: Calendars.

7. Structured

Best for: Planning your day as a visual timeline
Price: Free to download; Pro is offered monthly, yearly and as a lifetime purchase (see the app for prices).

Structured lays tasks and imported calendar events on one visual timeline so you can block out your day. It runs on iPhone, the web, Mac and Apple Watch, and includes weekly and monthly views, widgets, notifications and an AI planning assistant.

Skip it if: your schedule is mostly meetings and shared calendars. It is built around planning a day more than managing a full calendar. More: Structured.

8. Notion Calendar

Best for: People whose projects live in Notion
Price: Free.

Notion Calendar is free and connects to Google Calendar, Outlook and iCloud calendars. It can show Notion database items such as deadlines next to your events, includes built-in scheduling links, and offers time zone views and widgets on iPhone and iPad. It also runs on Mac and Windows.

Skip it if: you do not use Notion. Much of its appeal comes from that connection, and it is not a task manager. More: Notion Calendar.

9. TimeTree

Best for: Families and groups sharing one calendar
Price: Free; Premium is about $4.49 a month or $44.99 a year, and varies by region.

TimeTree is built for sharing. Create a calendar for your family, team or club, filter several together, and keep everyone on the same page. Premium adds file attachments, a vertical calendar view, pinned events and an ad-free experience.

Skip it if: you mostly manage your own schedule or want deep task and planning tools. More: TimeTree.

10. Akiflow

Best for: Time-blocking tasks from many tools
Price: $34 a month, or $19 a month billed yearly ($228 a year); 7-day trial, no free plan.

Akiflow collects tasks into one list and lets you schedule them on your calendar so tasks and meetings sit side by side. It has an AI planner, runs on iPhone, Apple Watch and web, and is aimed at people who juggle many sources of work.

Skip it if: you want a free or low-cost calendar. It is the most expensive app in this list. More: Akiflow.

How to choose

Start with the question of what slows you down. If it is entering events, pick an app with natural-language input. If it is juggling a calendar and a to-do list, pick one that combines them. If it is coordinating with other people, pick one built for sharing. And if nothing slows you down, the calendar already on your iPhone may be the right answer. Most of these apps are free to try, so test one for a week against your real schedule.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free calendar app for iPhone?

Apple Calendar is built in and free, and Google Calendar and Notion Calendar are free too. Chrona and Fantastical have free tiers, with paid plans for extra features.

Is Apple Calendar good enough?

For many people, yes. It syncs through iCloud, works with Google and Exchange accounts, and is built into iOS. If you want natural-language entry, tasks or habits in the same app, look at Chrona, Fantastical or Calendars by Readdle.

Which calendar apps let me type events in plain English?

Chrona, Fantastical and Calendars by Readdle all accept natural-language input. Type something like a meeting name, day and time and the app builds the event.

Which calendar app is best for families?

TimeTree is built around shared calendars for families and groups. Apple Calendar and Google Calendar also support sharing if everyone uses the same ecosystem.
