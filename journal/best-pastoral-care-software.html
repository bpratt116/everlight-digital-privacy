The best pastoral care software for churches in 2026

November 2026

If you are searching for church care software, you will find two very different kinds of tool. One kind is a full church management system (ChMS) that handles giving, check-in, volunteers, and a member directory, with care as one feature among many. The other kind is built around a single job: helping pastors and elders remember who needs a visit, a call, or a prayer.

Full disclosure: we make Shepherd. We've tried to be fair — every tool here is good at something. We checked every price below on the vendor's own site in November 2026, and we say "about" or "from" wherever a price depends on your church.

How we picked

  • Care fit. Can a pastor log a visit, a prayer request, or a follow-up quickly, and find it again later?
  • Note privacy. Can a sensitive note stay sensitive without being left out of the record?
  • Real cost. What does a church of 100 to 500 people actually pay, and what else comes bundled?
  • Honest scope. Does the tool do what the church needs, without a pile of modules nobody opens?

Quick picks

  • Best for a care-first tool with flat pricing: Shepherd
  • Best for a dedicated care app with a trial: Notebird
  • Best free people database: Planning Center People
  • Best for care plus giving and check-in at one price: Breeze (from Tithely)
  • Best for small churches that want everything priced by size: ChurchTrac
  • Best for large churches that want a full suite: Pushpay ChMS
  • Best for churches with technical staff: Rock RMS
  • Best for a tiny team on day one: a spreadsheet

1. Shepherd

Best for: pastors, elders, and care teams who want one place for people, notes, and prayer requests.
Price: Free for up to 100 people (1 staff user, 3 volunteer leaders). Core is $19 a month, or $190 a year, for up to 1,500 people and 5 staff. Team is $49 a month for up to 5,000 people. Enterprise is by quote. Source: pastorshepherd.com/pricing.

Shepherd is a web app built around the pastor's week. Each person has one profile with family, relationships, and care history. Prayer requests move from ask to answer in a shared view. Every note is marked shared, private, or confidential to clergy when you write it, and a "My People" view gives each elder their own flock. Pricing is a flat number per tier, not per member. Core adds care pathways, reminders, and Planning Center sync.

Skip it if: you need giving, check-in, or event registration in the same tool. Shepherd is care-focused, not a full ChMS. We wrote more about that difference in church management software vs. a CRM.

2. Notebird

Best for: churches that want a dedicated care app and are happy to pay per user.
Price: Just Me is $12 a month. Care Team starts at about $20 a month for two users, then falls per user as the team grows. A 14-day free trial is included. Source: notebird.app/pricing.

Notebird is the closest tool to Shepherd in purpose, and it is a good one. It records visits, prayers, and conversations, with an activity feed, tasks, a milestones feed for birthdays and bereavements, and care reports. It also connects with Breeze and Planning Center, and it can import a spreadsheet of your people list. Its pricing page lists a Small Church Scholarship for churches under 200 in weekly attendance.

Skip it if: you want a free tier to start on, or you would rather pay one flat price than a per-user price.

3. Planning Center People

Best for: churches that want a free, solid database of people.
Price: Free for every church. Texting is a separate add-on. Source: planningcenter.com/people.

People is a membership database with notes, forms, custom fields, and workflows that can move someone toward membership or a ministry role. You can store prayer requests and counseling notes, and forms can collect new requests. It also pairs with the rest of Planning Center for services, check-in, and giving. Most churches already know the brand, which helps when volunteers need to learn it.

Skip it if: you want a view built around a care team's rhythm. People is a database first, so care takes some setup.

4. Breeze (from Tithely)

Best for: churches that want care, giving, and check-in at one flat price.
Price: about $72 a month for church management with giving, with a 30-day trial. A bundle that adds worship tools, a church app, and a website is about $119 a month. Source: breezechms.com/pricing.

Breeze is a full ChMS with unlimited contacts and admin users, giving, check-in, volunteer scheduling, groups, events, member directories, and a mobile app. Follow-ups and automation are part of it. For a church that needs the whole office covered, one flat price is easy to plan around.

Skip it if: you only need a care log. You will pay for, and have to learn, a lot you will not use.

5. ChurchTrac

Best for: small churches that want a full toolset priced by the number of names.
Price: from $29 a month for 75 names, up to $119 a month for unlimited names. A 30-day trial is included. Source: churchtrac.com/pricing.

ChurchTrac includes people, giving, fund accounting, check-in, worship planning, groups, volunteers, and a website and app in every plan. Archived names do not count toward your limit. Messaging upgrades and online giving processing cost extra.

Skip it if: you want care to be the center of the tool. It is a broad suite, and care is one part of it.

6. Pushpay ChMS

Best for: larger churches that want a full suite and a sales team to help.
Price: by quote. Pushpay lists three tiers (Core, Advanced, Complete) with no dollar amounts. Source: pushpay.com/pricing.

Pushpay's ChMS tiers range from people and groups up to volunteer scheduling, check-in, forms, and multi-site management. Private notes and group leadership hierarchy appear in the top tier. It is built for churches that want giving and engagement tools in the same place as their records.

Skip it if: you want a price on a web page, or a smaller tool you can set up in an afternoon.

7. Rock RMS

Best for: churches with technical staff who want to shape their own system.
Price: no license fee. Rock suggests a donation of about $4.45 per weekend attendee per year, and hosting is extra and varies by provider. Source: rockrms.com/pricing.

Rock is open source and very customizable. It covers check-in, prayer, finance tools, communication, groups, and workflows, and it can be self-hosted or run by a hosting partner. Large churches use it for the control it gives them.

Skip it if: nobody on your team enjoys configuring software. Flexibility is also work.

8. A spreadsheet

Best for: a very small church, or the first month of getting organized.
Price: free.

We mean this honestly. A shared sheet with a last-contact date and an owner for each name beats memory. It is also easy to share and easy to leave.

Skip it if: more than one person writes care notes, or any of those notes are sensitive. Everyone with the link sees everything, and a sheet cannot show a history of care.

How to choose

Start with the problem. If your office needs giving, check-in, and scheduling, pick a full ChMS and judge its care features as a bonus. If your problem is that people go quiet and nobody notices, pick a care-focused tool. The two also work together: Planning Center and Breeze both connect to care apps like Notebird, and Shepherd's Core tier includes Planning Center sync.

Then test it with your actual team. Log a hospital visit from your phone and see how many taps it takes. If the answer is "too many," the tool will not get used.

Frequently asked questions

What is pastoral care software?

It is a tool for keeping a shared record of how your church cares for people: notes, visits, follow-ups, prayer requests, and who is responsible for whom. A full ChMS also handles giving and check-in, while care software focuses on the care itself.

Is there free church care software?

Yes. Planning Center People is free for every church, and Shepherd is free for up to 100 people. Rock RMS has no license fee, but hosting and setup cost something. A spreadsheet is free too, with real limits.

Do I need a full church management system for pastoral care?

Not always. If the office needs giving and check-in, a full system makes sense. If the pain is that pastors lose track of who needs a visit, a care-focused tool is often simpler and cheaper, and it can run beside a bigger system.

How do I keep pastoral notes private?

Look for per-note visibility, not just per-user permissions. Shepherd lets you mark each note shared, private, or confidential to clergy. Some full systems offer private notes on higher tiers. A shared spreadsheet cannot separate sensitive notes from routine ones.
