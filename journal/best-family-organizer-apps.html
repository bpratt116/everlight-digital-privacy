The best family organizer apps in 2026

November 2026

Every family ends up running on some mix of a shared calendar, a grocery note, a chore list and one person's memory. A family organizer app tries to put those pieces in one place so the whole household can see the same plan. There are a lot of them, and they are good at different things. This is a plain roundup of the best family calendar and organizer apps in 2026, with what each one does well and who should look elsewhere.

Full disclosure: we make Olive. We've tried to be fair — every app here is good at something.

How we picked

  • Built for more than one person. Every app here lets a household share the same plan, not just keep a private list.
  • Still alive and maintained. We checked each one is currently on the App Store or actively sold. A few older names did not make the cut because we could not confirm them.
  • Clear about cost. We list what each app charges as of November 2026, and say "about" when the price varies by plan or region.
  • Honest strengths. We left out apps we could not say anything specific and true about.

Quick picks

  • Best for tasks, meals and groceries in one iPhone app: Olive
  • Best long-running free family organizer: Cozi
  • Best all-in-one with finance and messaging extras: FamilyWall
  • Best for a shared calendar with lots of groups: TimeTree
  • Best for a wall display in the kitchen: Skylight
  • Best for groceries, recipes and meal plans only: AnyList
  • Best if you already live in Google: Google Calendar
  • Best free option on iPhones: Apple Family Sharing, Calendar and Reminders

1. Olive

Best for: families who want the week, the meals and the grocery list in one shared place on iPhone and iPad.
Price: Free to use. Olive Plus (the AI helpers) is $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year.

Olive is our app, so weigh this entry accordingly. It gives the household a single Today screen for tasks, routines and the calendar, then connects meals to groceries: plan the week's dinners and Olive builds the grocery list from them. You can type or dictate what is on your mind and it sorts the items into the right place, and everything you add is shared with the people you invite. We designed it without leaderboards or a lot of nagging notifications. If the load of running a home mostly lands on one person, our piece on the invisible mental load of running a household explains the thinking behind it.

Skip it if: your family uses Android, or you want a wall display or a long list of integrations. Olive is iOS only.

2. Cozi

Best for: a simple, long-running organizer with a color-coded family calendar and shared lists.
Price: Free with ads. Cozi Gold, which removes ads and adds extras, is about $30 a year.

Cozi pairs a color-coded calendar with shopping lists, to-do lists, a recipe box and automatic daily or weekly agenda emails. It works on iPhone, Android and in a browser. Gold adds a month view on mobile, calendar search, more reminders, a birthday tracker and an ad-free experience. It is a dependable pick for families who want something plain and widely used.

Skip it if: you want meal plans that feed straight into a grocery list, or you dislike ads in the free tier.

3. FamilyWall

Best for: families who want one app to cover calendar, lists, documents and messaging.
Price: Free to start. Premium is about $44.99 a year or $7.99 a month, with a 30-day free trial.

FamilyWall bundles a shared family calendar, shopping and task lists, document sharing, finance tracking, meal planning and private messaging. It runs on phones, tablets and any web browser. If you want one app that does as much as possible, this is the broadest option on the list.

Skip it if: you want a focused tool. A wide feature set means more to learn and more screens to set up.

4. TimeTree

Best for: couples and families who juggle several shared calendars, such as home, school and a sports team.
Price: Free to start. Premium is about $44.99 a year or $4.49 a month, and prices vary by region.

TimeTree is built around shared calendars you can switch between or view together. It adds notifications, memos and to-do lists, shared albums, and syncing with a device calendar such as Google Calendar. Premium adds file attachments, a vertical calendar view, pinned events and an ad-free experience. It is on iOS and Android.

Skip it if: you want meal planning or a grocery list tied to the calendar. It is a calendar first.

5. Skylight

Best for: families who want the plan on a wall display where everyone walks past it.
Price: The app is free. The Calendar device is sold separately, with prices that vary by model and retailer. The optional Plus subscription is $79 a year.

The Skylight app activates and manages the Skylight Calendar and Frame. The Calendar syncs unlimited calendars, supports recurring chores and routines, and shares grocery and to-do lists. Stars and rewards, meal plans and auto-importing of events and recipes are part of Plus. A screen on the wall is hard to ignore, which is the point.

Skip it if: you do not want to buy hardware. The app is mainly the control panel for the device.

6. AnyList

Best for: groceries, recipes and meal planning shared with a partner or the whole household.
Price: Free to start. AnyList Complete is $9.99 a year for one person or $14.99 a year for a household.

AnyList does one job and does it well. Lists sync in real time as people shop, it has a recipe box and a meal plan everyone can see, and it supports widgets and adding items by voice. Plenty of families pair it with a separate calendar app.

Skip it if: you want chores, routines or a family calendar in the same place. It is focused on food and shopping.

7. Google Calendar

Best for: families already on Google accounts who want a free, flexible shared calendar.
Price: Free.

You can share a calendar with specific people and choose a permission level, from seeing only free or busy time up to making changes and managing sharing. Everyone can add events from the apps they already use. It is the most flexible calendar here, though it has no built-in grocery or chore tools.

Skip it if: you want lists, meals or chores in the same app. Pair it with something else.

8. Apple Family Sharing

Best for: households where everyone has an iPhone and wants zero new apps to install.
Price: Free with the iPhone. Family Sharing itself has no separate fee, though shared subscriptions may cost extra.

Family Sharing lets an organizer share eligible subscriptions and purchases and, if each person opts in, location with up to five other family members. Calendar and Reminders can be shared through iCloud as well. It is private, fast and already on the phone in your pocket.

Skip it if: someone in the family uses Android, or you want meal planning, routines or a single Today view.

How to choose

Start with the problem that actually hurts. If it is double-booked weekends, a shared calendar like TimeTree or Google Calendar may be enough. If it is "what's for dinner and what do we need," look at AnyList or Olive. If you want everyone to see the plan without opening a phone, Skylight puts it on the wall. If you want one app that covers tasks, calendar, meals and groceries together, try Olive or FamilyWall.

Whatever you pick, the app only works if the whole household uses it. Try the free version for a week with everyone invited before you pay for anything.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best family calendar app?

It depends on how your family works. Google Calendar is the most flexible free option, TimeTree is strong for several shared calendars, and Cozi is a simple long-running favorite. If you want your calendar tied to tasks, meals and groceries, an all-in-one like Olive or FamilyWall fits better.

Is there a free family organizer app?

Yes. Cozi, FamilyWall, TimeTree, AnyList and Olive all have free versions, and Google Calendar and Apple's built-in apps are free. Paid tiers mostly add things like ad removal, more reminders or extra features.

What is the best app for family chores and routines?

Olive and Skylight both support household routines and chores, and Cozi lets you make chore checklists as to-do lists. Skylight adds rewards through its Plus plan. Pick the one that matches whether you want a phone app or a wall display.

Which family organizer works on both iPhone and Android?

Cozi, TimeTree and Google Calendar are available on both. Olive is currently iOS only.
