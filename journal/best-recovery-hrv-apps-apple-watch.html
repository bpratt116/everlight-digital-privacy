The best recovery and HRV apps for Apple Watch in 2026

November 2026

Your Apple Watch records HRV, resting heart rate and sleep every night. The hard part is turning those numbers into a decision: train hard today, or back off. A recovery app does that translation, and there are now plenty to choose from.

Full disclosure: we make Regen. We've tried to be fair — every app here is good at something. We looked at nine, and for each we checked the official site and the US App Store listing in November 2026. Prices change, so confirm before you subscribe. None of these apps is a medical device, and none can tell you whether you are healthy.

How we picked

  • It works with Apple Watch or Apple Health. Where an app needs its own band, ring or sensor, we say so.
  • It answers a recovery or readiness question. A score, a call, or a trend you can act on.
  • Price is clear. We list what we could verify and flag anything that varies.

Quick picks

  • Best for a simple daily call: Regen
  • Best WHOOP-style dashboard on your watch: Athlytic
  • Best all-in-one health app: Bevel
  • Best for low-pressure guidance: Gentler Streak
  • Best for endurance athletes: Training Today
  • Best for measured HRV data: HRV4Training
  • Best free HRV tracker (with a sensor): Elite HRV
  • Best dedicated band: WHOOP
  • Best ring: Oura

1. Regen

Best for: a straight daily answer, built on Apple Health data you already have
Price: Free to download. Paid Base and Elite tiers add deeper features (current prices are on the App Store listing).

Regen is ours, so weigh this entry accordingly. Each morning it reads your HRV, resting heart rate and sleep from Apple Health and gives you a 0-100 recovery score, a plain-English call (push, moderate, recover or rest), and one line on which number moved it. That part, plus a session suggestion, GPS tracking with routes and splits, and personal records, is free. Base adds run-and-lift-aware session guidance and longer trends. Elite adds a coach you can ask questions. It reads data on your device and needs Apple Health. For background on the metric itself, see our piece on HRV for athletes.

Skip it if: you want a huge dashboard of scores, nutrition logging or a social feed. Regen is deliberately narrow, and it is iPhone only.

2. Athlytic

Best for: people who want a WHOOP-style recovery and exertion view from an Apple Watch
Price: Free to download. Athlytic Pro is $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year in the US App Store listing.

Athlytic compares your overnight HRV and resting heart rate with your own baseline for a daily recovery score, adds a 0 to 10 exertion score with a target range, and suggests workouts you can send to your watch. It also covers sleep, stress and a fitness-age number. It has a long track record on the App Store and a large review base.

Skip it if: you want something minimal. There is a lot on the screen, and the best parts sit behind the subscription.

3. Bevel

Best for: an all-in-one health app: recovery, sleep, strain and nutrition
Price: Free to download. Bevel Pro lists at $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year in the US App Store; AI credit packs are sold separately.

Bevel pulls from Apple Watch and Apple Health and presents recovery, sleep and strain scores, nutrition logging with barcode scanning, and an AI health coach called Bevel Intelligence. If you want one app for the whole picture, it is among the most complete options here.

Skip it if: you only want a morning recovery number. You would pay for, and scroll past, a lot you don't need.

4. Gentler Streak

Best for: people who want gentle, low-pressure guidance, including on rest days
Price: Free to download. Premium has monthly, yearly and lifetime options; the US listing shows monthly at $8.99 and yearly at $39.99.

Gentler Streak reads your activity, sleep and vitals from Apple Health and suggests whether today suits a walk, a workout or rest. Rest days keep your streak alive, and there are no rings to close. Its makers say data stays on your device, with no account. It has won Apple design recognition, and it is built for all kinds of bodies and energy levels.

Skip it if: you are chasing performance numbers. It is intentionally light on hard-training detail.

5. Training Today

Best for: triathletes, runners, cyclists and swimmers who want readiness tied to a plan
Price: Free to download. Premium lists at $2.95 a month or $29.99 a year in the US App Store.

Training Today turns HRV readings from Apple Health and your personal baseline into a Readiness to Train score. Premium adds structured swim, bike and run workouts that adapt to it, weekly planning, and connections to TrainingPeaks and ROUVY.

Skip it if: you lift or do mixed training. It is aimed at endurance athletes.

6. HRV4Training

Best for: people who want a measured morning HRV reading and the data behind it
Price: About $9.99 to download. HRV4Training Pro is a $59.99 in-app purchase in the US App Store.

HRV4Training is built by a researcher and describes itself as a scientifically validated HRV app. You take a one-minute morning measurement with your phone camera or a chest strap, or use night data from some devices. It shows your range, lets you tag things like alcohol and travel, and finds patterns. Our reading of its listing: it measures on its own rather than simply reading your watch.

Skip it if: you want zero effort. A daily one-minute measurement is the whole idea, and it is a habit you must keep.

7. Elite HRV

Best for: free HRV tracking with an external sensor
Price: The core app is free for personal use; its listing shows add-ons from $0.99 to $4.99.

Elite HRV has been a staple for athletes for years. It tracks HRV, stress and recovery and lists a wide range of compatible sensors. The key point for this article: it requires an HRV-capable sensor, so it is not a plug-and-play Apple Watch app.

Skip it if: you want to wear only your watch. Buy a compatible chest strap or sensor first.

8. WHOOP

Best for: people who want a dedicated, screenless band and don't mind a membership
Price: From about $199 a year (WHOOP One) in the US; higher tiers cost more. WHOOP's site also shows limited-time promotions.

WHOOP is hardware. The app needs a WHOOP wearable, and you pay a membership that includes it. It delivers sleep, strain, recovery and stress insights around the clock, and the first month is listed as a free trial. Because it is a separate band, it is a different decision than the Apple Watch apps above.

Skip it if: you want to use the Apple Watch you already own, or avoid a yearly membership.

9. Oura

Best for: people who prefer a ring for sleep and readiness
Price: Ring purchase plus membership, listed at $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year in the US.

Oura needs its own ring. Its app gives daily Sleep, Activity and Readiness scores and tracks more than 50 health metrics, according to its membership page. Many people like a ring for overnight comfort.

Skip it if: you don't want to buy a ring, or you want to build around your Apple Watch.

How to choose

Start with hardware. If you already wear an Apple Watch, begin with the first six apps; WHOOP and Oura mean buying a new device, and Elite HRV needs a sensor. Then decide how much you want to see. If a single morning answer is enough, pick a simple app. If you like digging into trends, pick a deeper one. Most have a free tier, so try one for a couple of weeks. HRV only makes sense against your own baseline, so give any app time before you judge it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best HRV app for Apple Watch?

There is no single best one. Athlytic and Bevel are the closest to a full WHOOP-style dashboard on a watch you already own. Training Today and Regen aim at a simple daily training call. HRV4Training suits people who want a validated morning measurement and a deeper look at their data. Pick based on how much detail you want and what you are willing to pay.

Do I need a WHOOP or Oura to get a recovery score?

No. Several apps here read the HRV, resting heart rate and sleep your Apple Watch already records through Apple Health and turn them into a recovery or readiness number. WHOOP and Oura are different: each needs its own band or ring, plus a membership.

Is a free recovery app good enough?

Often, yes. Regen's daily recovery score, push-or-rest call and short explanation are free. Elite HRV's core app is free for personal use but needs a compatible HRV sensor. Paid tiers mostly add deeper trends, structured workouts or coaching.

How accurate is HRV from an Apple Watch?

HRV is a noisy signal, and one morning tells you little. What matters is how your readings compare with your own baseline over a week or two. These apps are training aids, not medical devices, and none of them can diagnose anything. Talk to a clinician about health concerns.
