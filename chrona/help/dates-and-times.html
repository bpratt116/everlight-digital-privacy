Dates and times

How Chrona reads “friday”, “6”, “3-4pm” and “in 3 days”

Ways to say a date

  • Relative: today, tomorrow, in 3 days, next week. Shorthands work too: tmrw, tmw, eod (end of day), eow (end of week).
  • Weekdays: friday, next tuesday.
  • Calendar dates: 6/12, June 12, Jun 12.
  • Spans: June 1-3 makes a three-day event.

Two rules that surprise people

A bare weekday means the next one. If today is Tuesday and you type tuesday, Chrona schedules a week out, not today — because if you meant today you would have said “today”.

A date that already passed rolls forward a year. Typing January 4 in November gives you next January, not one ten months gone.

Ways to say a time

  • at 2pm, at 9:30am, at noon, at midnight
  • 3-4pm or from 9am to 5pm for a range
  • for 30 min / for 2 hours to set a length instead of an end time

The bare-hour rule

A bare hour from 1 to 7 is assumed to be PM. “Gym at 6” means 6 in the evening.

Almost nobody schedules a 6 AM meeting by typing “at 6”, so Chrona takes the reading you almost certainly meant. If you did mean the morning, say at 6am. Hours 8 through 12 are read literally, because “at 9” is genuinely ambiguous and morning is the safer guess there.

Ranges need the meridiem on the end

Write 3-4pm, not 3-4. Without an am/pm anywhere in the range, Chrona cannot tell a time range from a date range or a page number, so it leaves the text alone rather than guessing. An event with no end time is one hour long.
