Tasks, projects and the inbox

When a sentence becomes a task instead of an event

Three ways to get a task

  1. Leave out the time. Call mom tomorrow is a task due tomorrow. This is the common case and needs no special syntax at all.
  2. Start with a task word. todo, task, or reminder at the front forces a task even when you give a time.
  3. Add a #tag or a >Project. Either one keeps the sentence a task, because you would not file a calendar event under a project.

Filing as you type

todo: email invoice #work #urgent >Billing
todo Ship it !high
todo: renew passport friday !high #travel
  • #tag — any number of tags, created on first use
  • >Project — files the task under a project
  • ! low, !! medium, !!! high — or the word forms !low, !med, !high

The inbox

A task with no date lands in your inbox rather than being forced onto a day you have not chosen. That is deliberate: capture is supposed to be fast, and deciding when is a separate act from deciding what. Empty the inbox whenever you like by giving those tasks a day.

Deadlines versus dates

A due date says when a task is due. A planned duration — todo Call dentist tomorrow at 2pm for 30 min — says how long you expect it to take, which is what lets Chrona block it out on your timeline alongside your real events.
