Quick Add syntax reference

Every phrase Chrona understands, and what each one produces

This page is generated directly from the app's own example catalog, and every phrase on it is re-parsed by Chrona's test suite on each build. If a phrase stops working, this page fails to build rather than quietly going stale.

Rows marked as tasks create a to-do; the rest create calendar events. The difference is almost always whether the sentence contains a clock time — see Getting started.

Dates

Call mom tomorrow
A task due tomorrow — no clock time means a task, not an event
Dentist 6/12 3pm
June 12 at 3 PM
Trip June 1-3
A three-day span
Follow up in 3 days
Counts forward from today
  • A date with no clock time makes a TASK due that day, not an event. Add a time — "Call mom tomorrow at 2pm" — to put it on the calendar.
  • A bare weekday means the NEXT one. Naming today rolls a full week ahead.
  • A month and day that already passed rolls forward to next year.
  • Shorthands work too: tmrw, tmw, eod (end of day), eow (end of week).

Times & ranges

Lunch at noon
Today at 12 PM
Gym at 6
6 PM — bare hours 1–7 assume PM
3-4pm meeting
3 PM to 4 PM
Team offsite from 9am to 5pm tomorrow at HQ
A full workday tomorrow, at HQ
  • A bare hour from 1 to 7 is assumed to be PM — "Gym at 6" means 6 PM.
  • A range needs the meridiem on the end: "3-4pm", not "3-4".
  • No end time means one hour.

Durations

Deep work tomorrow at 8am for 2 hours
8 AM to 10 AM tomorrow
todo Call dentist tomorrow at 2pm for 30 min
A task with 30 minutes planned
  • "for 30 min" sets the length instead of an end time.
  • On a task, the duration becomes its planned time so it can be blocked out.

Repeating

Standup every weekday at 9:30am
Monday–Friday at 9:30 AM
Pay rent every month
A repeating task, once a month
Board meeting every 2nd Tuesday at 10am
The second Tuesday of each month
Standup first Monday of the month at 9am
The first Monday of each month
Standup every weekday at 9:30am until August 14
Repeats through August 14, then stops
Standup every weekday at 9:30am for 6 times
Repeats six times, then stops
  • "every weekday" is Monday through Friday.
  • Ordinals work: "first Monday of the month", "every 2nd Tuesday".
  • End it with "until <date>" (that day is included) or "for 6 times". With neither, it repeats forever.

Alerts

Flight 6/12 at 6am alert 2 hours before
Alerts at 4 AM on June 12
Standup tomorrow at 9am alert 15 min before
Alerts at 8:45 AM tomorrow
  • Alerts are handed to the calendar itself, so they fire like any other event alert.
  • Say the amount and the word before: "alert 2 hours before".

Calendars

/work Review PRs tomorrow at 9
Saved to your Work calendar
/personal Dentist friday at 3pm
Saved to your Personal calendar
  • A leading /name picks the calendar to save to.
  • The name must match one of your calendars, otherwise it stays in the title.

Tasks

todo: call plumber
An undated task in your inbox
todo Buy milk friday
A task due Friday
todo: email invoice #work #urgent >Billing
Tagged work and urgent, filed under Billing
  • Starting with todo, task, or reminder makes a task instead of an event.
  • You often don't need the prefix: anything without a clock time is a task already. A #tag or >project keeps it a task even when you do give it a time.
  • #tag files it under a tag; >Project files it under a project.
  • A task with no date lands in your inbox.

Locations

Coffee at starbucks tomorrow
A task tomorrow, at Starbucks — add a time to make it an event
Lunch with Emily tomorrow at 12pm at Texas Roadhouse
Noon tomorrow, at Texas Roadhouse
  • "at <place>" after the time sets the location.
  • A person's name after "with" is kept in the title — it is never treated as a place.

Priority

todo Ship it !high
A high-priority task
todo: renew passport friday !high #travel
High priority, due Friday, tagged travel
  • ! is low, !! is medium, !!! is high.
  • The word forms work too: !low, !med, !high.
